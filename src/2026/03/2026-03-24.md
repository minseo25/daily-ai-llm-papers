### 1. On the Direction of RLVR Updates for LLM Reasoning: Identification and Exploitation

**Track**: RL for LLMs / Reasoning | **Link**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22117)

📋 Problem Definition

While RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) is known to improve LLM reasoning abilities, prior work has focused only on the "magnitude" of changes induced by RLVR while overlooking their "direction." This paper analyzes the directionality of token-level probability changes between a base model and an RLVR model to identify precisely where and how RLVR improves reasoning, and proposes practical performance improvement techniques that exploit this directional information.

📚 Background / Related Works

Wang et al. (2025b) observed that RLVR changes are sparse and concentrated on high-entropy tokens, while Huan et al. (2025, UniReason) measured KL divergence between base and RLVR models to confirm the sparsity of changes. Yang et al. (2025b) and Deng et al. (2025) analyzed that this sparsity stems from selective gradient updates during RLVR training. Algorithmically, GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and its improvement DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) have become the core techniques for critic-free RLVR, with this paper using DAPO as the primary baseline. Meng et al. (2026)'s cross-sample token exchange experiments provide the direct foundation for the selective token replacement framework proposed in this paper.

🔬 Main Methodology

The core contributions are three-fold. First, the paper proposes token-level signed log-probability difference Δlog p = log π_RL(y_t) - log π_Base(y_t) as the key metric for RLVR analysis. Magnitude-based metrics like entropy and KL divergence fail to distinguish between base and RLVR models because their histograms are nearly identical, but Δlog p exhibits a clear bimodal distribution with a positive tail (tokens preferred by RLVR) and a negative tail (tokens preferred by the base model). Second, the paper proposes a test-time extrapolation technique that further extrapolates the RLVR model's distribution in the Δlog p direction, decoding with an enhanced policy defined as log π_Extra = (1+γ)·log π_RL - γ·log π_Base. Third, for training-time, based on the observation that tokens with high Δlog p correspond to low-probability tokens, the paper proposes a probability-aware reweighting technique that reweights the policy gradient advantage inversely proportional to token probability. Theoretically, Lemma 3.1 proves that the L1-norm of the gradient of the DAPO objective with respect to logits is 2|w_{i,t}|·(1-π_θ(y_{i,t})), formalizing the mechanism by which low-probability tokens receive disproportionately large gradients.

🧪 Evaluation

Selective token replacement experiments were conducted on the AIME-24 dataset across multiple RLVR-base model pairs including ORZ, DAPO, and UniReason. Δlog p-based selection recovered RLVR model accuracy by replacing only ~10% of tokens, whereas divergence-based selection required >20% and entropy-based required even more. Top-p filtering experiments showed that restricting to top-p=0.95 alone significantly degraded DAPO training performance, demonstrating the causal importance of low-probability tokens. Training-time reweighting achieved additional reasoning performance gains over DAPO across various benchmarks and models (including Qwen2.5-Math-7B), and test-time extrapolation exceeded RLVR model accuracy without any additional training.

💡 Key Intuition & Lesson

RLVR improves reasoning not by broadly shifting the entire distribution, but by directionally redistributing probability mass at a small number of low-probability token positions. These tokens act as "branching points" in the reasoning process, and the directional information — invisible to magnitude-based analysis — is the key signal. Practically, understanding the directionality of RLVR training enables performance gains at test-time through extrapolation without additional training, and at training-time by concentrating learning resources on low-probability tokens to maximize efficiency. This is the most important lesson from this paper.

### 2. PivotRL: High Accuracy Agentic Post-Training at Low Compute Cost

**Track**: LLM Post-training | **Link**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21383)

📋 Problem Definition

In post-training for long-horizon agentic tasks, SFT is computationally efficient but suffers severe OOD (out-of-domain) performance degradation, while E2E RL maintains OOD generalization but requires multi-turn on-policy rollouts per parameter update, making it prohibitively expensive. This paper seeks to simultaneously achieve SFT's data efficiency and E2E RL's generalization ability while obtaining high accuracy without full-trajectory rollouts.

📚 Background / Related Works

The paper uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization, Shao et al., 2024) as the base RL objective, and employs agentic benchmarks including SWE-Bench (Jimenez et al., 2024), tau^2-Bench (Xie et al., 2024), web search (Yao et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2025), and tool use (Schick et al., 2023). SFT's OOD performance degradation is documented in Chu et al. (2025) and Luo et al. (2025b), while E2E RL's OOD preservation is confirmed in Ouyang et al. (2022) and Chen et al. (2025) among others. PivotRL adopts a local RL paradigm that reuses existing SFT trajectories for RL, focusing on overcoming the limitations of simple exact-match reward approaches.

🔬 Main Methodology

PivotRL's core consists of two mechanisms. First, for all turn candidates extracted from SFT trajectories, K local rollouts are performed with the reference policy, and only turns with reward variance greater than 0 and mean reward below a difficulty threshold are selected as "pivots." The empirical observation that 71% of random turns have zero reward variance (all successes or all failures) and thus carry no learning signal motivates this filtering. Second, instead of a strict reward that only rewards exact string matches to SFT demonstrations, domain-specific verifiers award reward to functionally equivalent actions. Theoretically, Theorem 3.2 proves that GRPO's natural gradient norm is proportional to reward standard deviation, providing justification for filtering mixed-outcome turns, and Theorem 3.3 shows that the optimal policy under functional-reward-based RL preserves the relative ordering of the reference policy within each of the acceptable and unacceptable action sets, providing the theoretical basis for maintaining OOD performance. During training, the only online interactions required are single-turn rollouts from selected pivot states, dramatically reducing compute compared to full-trajectory rollouts.

🧪 Evaluation

Using the same training data (same prompts, same expert trajectories), PivotRL achieved an average in-domain accuracy gain of +14.11pp across 4 domains (interactive tool use, agentic coding, terminal tool use, and search) compared to SFT's +9.94pp, while maintaining OOD performance on non-agentic domains (math, science QA, competitive coding) with only +0.21pp change — essentially no degradation (SFT regressed -9.48pp). On SWE-Bench, PivotRL achieved accuracy competitive with E2E RL while using only one quarter of the rollout turns. PivotRL was deployed in production-scale agentic post-training of NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B alongside SFT and E2E RL as a primary training method.

💡 Key Intuition & Lesson

The core insight is that "not all turns are equally useful." In group-normalized RL like GRPO, turns with zero reward variance (all successes or all failures) produce exactly zero gradient, so concentrating rollout budget on "pivot" turns — where outcomes are mixed — maximizes learning efficiency per unit of compute. Furthermore, in generative action spaces, rewards must be relaxed from exact string matching to functional equivalence; doing so theoretically preserves the relative ordering of task-irrelevant actions, structurally preventing OOD performance degradation. This presents a practical path to gaining RL's generalization benefits with minimal additional compute in real-world settings where SFT data already exists.

### 3. CommCP: Efficient Multi-Agent Coordination via LLM-Based Communication with Conformal Prediction

**Track**: Agents | **Link**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06038)

📋 Problem Definition

This paper addresses the problem of multiple heterogeneous robots in a home environment, each performing different tasks while cooperatively gathering information to answer questions given in natural language. This is formalized as a new problem called Multi-Agent Multi-Task Embodied Question Answering (MM-EQA). The key challenge is filtering out unnecessary or misleading information from inter-robot communication to improve exploration efficiency. Simply parallelizing single-agent EQA leads to redundant exploration, and sharing raw uncalibrated LLM outputs degrades performance.

📚 Background / Related Works

In single-agent EQA, "Explore until Confident" (EuC) proposed using conformal prediction to continue exploration until the VLM's confidence is sufficient; CommCP extends this approach's planning and confidence-checking modules. In LLM-based distributed multi-agent coordination, peer-to-peer communication via natural language text has been studied, but the lack of mechanisms to evaluate communication quality made it inefficient. While the application of conformal prediction to robot planning has been addressed in prior work such as KnowNo, this paper is the first to apply it to communication calibration between multiple agents.

🔬 Main Methodology

CommCP is a distributed framework consisting of four modules: perception, communication, planning, and confidence check. The core of the communication module is LLM-based object relevance reasoning, where the robot evaluates the relevance between observed objects and partner robots' target objects using four options: A (same object), B (high relevance), C (low relevance), D (general feature). To address LLM overconfidence, split conformal prediction is applied — computing probability thresholds for options A and B each based on a calibration dataset collected from 20 HM3D scenarios, and including only judgments exceeding these thresholds in messages. Calibrated relevant/target object information included in messages is reflected in semantic values (SV) of a 2D grid map, guiding robots in frontier-based exploration (FBE) to prioritize information-rich areas. The framework also includes a mechanism where, if the product of VLM answer probability and question-image relevance score exceeds 1-ε₂, the answer is finalized and transmitted to the responsible robot if the question was not its own.

🧪 Evaluation

An MM-EQA benchmark was constructed and evaluated in photorealistic home environments based on the HM3D dataset, with success rate (SC) and normalized completion time (NTC) as primary metrics. Results in Figure 3 show that CommCP significantly improves task success rate and substantially reduces completion time compared to baselines of independent exploration without communication and raw LLM output sharing without conformal prediction, and maintains stable performance across message transmission rate variations (0.25~4 msg/s). Scalability analysis to 3-robot teams also confirmed that CommCP consistently outperforms baselines.

💡 Key Intuition & Lesson

The core insight of this paper is that "agents should only share information they are confident is highly relevant to their partner's task" — empirically demonstrating that indiscriminate information sharing actually reduces cooperative efficiency. Applying conformal prediction, a statistical calibration tool, to LLM communication message generation to obtain probabilistic guarantees (marginal coverage guarantee) is a meaningful extension showing that confidence calibration in LLM-based multi-agent systems can be effectively applied not only to individual agent decision-making but also to quality control of inter-agent communication channels. The practical lesson is that leveraging LLMs' world knowledge about spatial co-occurrence patterns of objects in home environments — while calibrating their outputs to reduce noise — is key to effective multi-robot cooperation.
